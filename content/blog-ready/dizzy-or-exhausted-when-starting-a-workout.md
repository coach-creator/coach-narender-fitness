If you feel dizzy or exhausted when starting a workout, stop, and sit or lie down. Mayo Clinic's getting-started guidance is plain about this: if you feel pain, shortness of breath, dizziness or nausea, take a break, because you may be pushing yourself too hard. The NHS adds that dizziness, feeling lightheaded or feeling off-balance is common and not usually serious, and that you should see a GP if you're worried.

So I won't tell you to push through it. I look at sleep and food, and the hard work stays off until a doctor has ruled out what a trainer shouldn't guess.

This is general education from a personal trainer, not medical advice. Back pain, chest pain, dizziness, a joint that locks, pain down the leg, or a condition a doctor already follows needs a doctor first. Narender sends clients for imaging and an orthopaedic opinion when that is the right step, then trains with the doctor's guidance.

## What to do if you are dizzy or exhausted when starting a workout

Stop the set first. The NHS dizziness page has a short self-care list, starting with lying down until it passes, getting up slowly, moving slowly and resting, and the page itself covers the rest. What I'd underline from it are the don'ts. Don't bend down suddenly, and don't stand up suddenly after sitting or lying down. Don't drive, climb a ladder or use heavy machinery while you're dizzy.

The same page says not to self-diagnose, even when another symptom gives you a hunch, and asks you to see a GP if:

- you are worried, or the dizziness will not go away or keeps coming back
- hearing or speech is harder, or there is ringing in the ears
- vision changes, or the face, arms, or legs feel numb or weak
- your pulse changes, you faint or collapse, you have headaches, or you are being sick

Some signs go past a GP visit. If the dizziness comes with severe trouble breathing, a tight or heavy chest, pain that spreads to the arms, back, neck and jaw, lips or skin turning very pale, blue or grey, or sudden confusion, the NHS shortness of breath page says to get emergency help. Mayo Clinic adds fainting, an upset stomach, blue lips or nails, and a change in mental alertness alongside shortness of breath as reasons for emergency care. In India, use your local emergency number or go to the nearest hospital emergency.

## Why do I feel lightheaded during morning workouts?

Picture an early alarm, no real breakfast, and a first set that makes the room tilt. That's a bad moment to insist the session still happen as written.

The NHS lists situations where dizziness shows up alongside something else, among them stress and anxiety, low blood sugar in diabetes, and iron deficiency anaemia. Its table also says dizziness when you stand or sit up suddenly can be a sudden drop in blood pressure, and dizziness after a new prescription can be a side effect of the medicine. That's one reason I ask about medicines in every assessment. I do not tell you to stop a prescription.

For people starting out, Mayo Clinic suggests warming up with easy walking or gentle stretching, building to a pace you can keep, and adding no more than 10% a week. With an injury or a medical condition, it says to talk with a healthcare professional first. The NHS activity page asks you to speak to your GP first if you haven't exercised for some time, or if you have a medical condition or a concern. A morning that already feels faint is a poor time for me to add load.

With one client, the early dizziness and blood pressure trouble traced back to poor sleep and food, and pushing harder would have been the wrong move. I don't assume every rough morning is that same picture, and a doctor still has to rule the other causes out. Until that's clear, the hard work stops. Once it is, the session stays at basic mobility and the load waits. Dheere dheere: slowly, on purpose.

## How poor sleep and high stress affect exercise

When projects pile up, I often see stress high enough to wreck sleep. The training gets irregular because the week does, and food slips at the same time: a carb-heavy lunch, often ordered in, and protein that stays low even after people have been told.

> Body aapke work schedule ke hisaab se kaam nahi karti. Your body does not work according to your work schedule.

On a week when sleep is short and the calendar is mean, I don't use the session as a test of character. Mayo Clinic says it's fine to take a day or two off when you're not feeling good, and that many people quit because they went too long and too hard, then got sore or injured. So on those days I keep basic mobility, plus yoga or meditation where it helps. The harder sets come back when sleep and food are steady again.

A missed morning doesn't have to be a lost day. The session can move to the evening, as long as you're not still dizzy and a doctor hasn't told you to rest. You pick the days and stay available, and I come to the house. We start with basic mobility, and I don't add a punishing set to make up for the missed alarm.

People do find an hour for a show and still say there's no time for the body. I ask where the evening went, the show after dinner included, because that's one way sleep gets shorter. Even then, the next session still has to be safe.
