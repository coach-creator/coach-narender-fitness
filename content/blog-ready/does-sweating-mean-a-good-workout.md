Does sweating mean a good workout? No. Sweating is just your body's temperature going up and it cooling itself down. A soaked shirt only tells you that you got warm. It says nothing about whether the exercises were right for you, or how many calories you burned.

This is general education from a personal trainer, not medical advice. Picture two Saturdays. On the first, you spend 45 minutes jumping through a circuit on a humid morning and go home with your T-shirt dripping. On the second, you do slow squats, some hip mobility and a few careful rows in an air-conditioned room, and you barely sweat. Most people would call the first one the better workout, and the shirt is the only reason they'd say so.

## Does sweating mean a good workout, or just a hot room?

Cleveland Clinic and Mayo Clinic describe sweat in almost the same words: it's the body's way of cooling itself. When you get too warm, your nervous system switches on the eccrine glands, which cover most of your skin. What they release is about 99% water, with mostly sodium and chloride in the rest. As it evaporates, it cools the skin and the tissue underneath.

Those glands also respond to strong emotion, which is why you can sweat before a difficult call without moving at all. And sweat isn't the body's main route for getting rid of toxins. Your liver and kidneys do that job, so sweating hard to "cleanse" yourself is unnecessary.

Sometimes the sweat itself is worth a doctor's look. Mayo Clinic suggests seeing a clinician for a sudden increase in sweating, sweating that disrupts daily life, night sweats with no clear cause, or an unexplained change in odour.

## Does more sweat mean more fat burned?

According to Cleveland Clinic, the amount you sweat doesn't change how many calories you burn. Making sweat costs very little energy. Harder muscle work is what raises the count, which is why a run in cool weather can burn hundreds of calories while you sweat relatively little.

How much you sweat depends on genetics, body size, fitness level, age, clothing, humidity and the temperature around you. So a June workout in a room without AC will soak you faster than the same workout on a December morning.

The weight you lose right after is mostly water, and much of it comes back once you rehydrate. Fat loss comes from a calorie deficit over time. Changes in body composition come from the muscle work, not from the sweat.

## Why a sweaty hour feels like a good one

A lot of people arrive and hand the hour over. In their mind, the trainer is the person who will change their body, and whatever is asked, they'll do. After a while the basic work starts to feel dull, and they want the session to entertain them as well.

> Aap class le rahe ho, entertainment ke liye nahi aa rahe. You are taking a class. You did not come to be entertained.

Some trainers answer that wish with jumping, CrossFit-type work and circuits. The sweat shows up, everyone takes it as the sign the hour worked, and the trainer tires you out and sends you home.

## How to tell if a workout worked without sweating

Ask what each exercise is for, and why. You should be able to say the reason back in ordinary words. Cleveland Clinic's advice is to judge regular exercise by how you feel, so over the weeks, notice that rather than how wet the shirt was.

Plenty of sessions fail that test. When I was on a gym team, I watched trainers open YouTube in the morning, look up five exercises someone had called the best, and give every client the same pattern. They hadn't taken a medical history, and yoga, rehab, sport, bodybuilding and powerlifting, which are different jobs, got mixed together in the same list.

A lot of people in this work never studied it. They liked training, built a body and started coaching. I won't call every trainer wrong, and some do take a medical history. But the industry is unorganised and nobody comes to check the hour, so you have to ask.

## Heavy strength can wait

I don't start anyone on heavy strength. First I need to understand the muscle imbalance, keep about 5 to 10 minutes of mobility in the session and look after the heart and lungs. The heavier work comes after that.

Many people want the other order. They want about 5% of the fat gone and to look better than before, and they set joint health aside: who needs to climb, who's going to run in an emergency? I put joint health and function first, and looks follow. Older people fracture bones slipping on stairs and wet floors, and keeping muscle and a healthy heart is how you stay independent and avoid a needless injury.
