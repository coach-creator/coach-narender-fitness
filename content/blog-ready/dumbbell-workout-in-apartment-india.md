A dumbbell workout in an apartment in India can train every major muscle group, with a pair of dumbbells, a mat and about two metres of floor. You do not need a squat rack. Mayo Clinic lists dumbbells among the classic strength training tools, and says about 20 to 30 minutes, twice a week, can be enough to notice stronger muscles.

This is general education from a personal trainer, not medical advice. ## What a pair of dumbbells covers, and what it does not

The NHS and the CDC both ask adults to do strength work on at least two days a week that covers the major muscle groups: legs, hips, back, abdomen, chest, shoulders and arms. With dumbbells you can load a squat or a lunge for legs and hips, a row for the back, a press for chest and shoulders, and carry them for your grip and middle.

A rack lets you put a heavy bar on your back. Dumbbells top out sooner, because you have to hold them, and that is the real limit of training in a flat. By the time a goblet squat with your heaviest dumbbell feels easy, you will know enough about your body to decide what comes next.

## Picking the right dumbbell weight

Mayo Clinic's rule is simple. Choose a weight heavy enough to tire the muscle after about 12 to 15 repetitions, and when you can easily do more, go up a little. It also says one hard set at that weight can build muscle in most people, so you do not need five sets of everything.

The NHS gives the same idea from the other side: strength work counts when you do it to the point where you need a short rest before going again. So if your last two reps of a row feel like the first two, the dumbbell is too light for that exercise.

In practice, one pair will be right for your arms and wrong for your legs, because legs are stronger. Say you buy a pair that suits your shoulder press; the squats may soon feel easy with it. One way round this is to buy for the squat and do fewer, cleaner reps on the presses, or to start with an adjustable pair. Either way, go up dheere dheere, slowly, one small step at a time.

## A dumbbell workout plan for an apartment in India

Mayo Clinic suggests warming up with a few minutes of brisk walking or other light aerobic activity, and resting one full day between sessions that work the same muscles. For a busy week that points to two full-body sessions with at least a day between them, say Tuesday and Friday.

When a founder tells me they have about three hours in a whole week, my answer is 30 to 40 minutes of strength, about 10 minutes of mobility warm-up so the joints move well, and a short finisher, split across the week. On one day, about 30 minutes of moderate cardio plus mobility. A pair of dumbbells handles the strength part of that.

One session might run like this, each move for one or two hard sets:

- goblet squat, holding one dumbbell at your chest
- one-arm row, with the other hand on a sturdy chair or the sofa arm
- floor press, lying on the mat
- reverse lunge, a dumbbell in each hand
- overhead press, standing or seated
- farmer's carry, walking the length of the hall and back

That is the shape, not your prescription. I do not hand out random lists, and every exercise in a plan I write is there for a reason I explain before you do it. If your knees complain on lunges or a shoulder does not like pressing overhead, the list changes.

## Training in a flat without annoying the floor below

Apartment life adds its own rules. A mat protects marble or vitrified tiles from a dropped dumbbell, protects your knees during lunges, and stops your feet sliding. Lower the weights to the floor at the end of a set instead of letting them fall. Your neighbour downstairs has probably had a long day too.

Jumping work is the other thing to skip indoors. Nothing in the list above needs a jump, and controlled reps keep your form clean.

Where you keep the pair decides how often you use it. Dumbbells kept in a basket by the sofa get used after an 8 pm finish. Dumbbells kept on top of the wardrobe become a place to hang clothes.

## Before you load up

I do not put anyone straight into heavy strength work. First I want to understand muscle imbalances, then add about 10 minutes of mobility, and keep an eye on heart and lung health. Joints and how you function come before how you look. There is more on that order in [mobility before heavy lifting](/blog/mobility-before-heavy-lifting/).

At home nobody is watching your form. Mayo Clinic advises anyone new to strength training to learn correct form with a trainer or other fitness specialist, and to breathe through each rep. If an exercise causes pain, its advice is to stop, then try a lower weight or come back to it in a few days.

The NHS says to speak to your doctor first if you have not exercised for some time, or if you have a medical condition or concern.
