Exercise snacks during workday sitting, the short bursts of movement you fit between meetings, are worth doing because they break up long hours in the chair. On their own, they are unlikely to cancel out a whole day of sitting. The Mayo Clinic describes research in which 60 to 75 minutes a day of moderate activity offset the effects of too much sitting, and a few two-minute breaks do not add up to that.

This is general education from a personal trainer, not medical advice. Picture a Tuesday. A 9 am stand-up, calls back to back till lunch, dal-chawal at the desk, then a 3 pm review that runs over. The first time you properly stand up is at 6. Ten squats beside the chair at 11 would not have saved that day, but they would have broken it into smaller pieces.

## What exercise snacks during workday sitting can and cannot do

Start with why sitting is the problem. The Mayo Clinic explains that you use less energy sitting than standing or moving, and links long sitting with obesity and with metabolic syndrome, a cluster of conditions that includes higher blood pressure, high blood sugar, extra fat around the waist and unhealthy cholesterol. It also reports a review of 13 studies covering more than a million people. Those who sat more than eight hours a day with no physical activity had a risk of dying similar to the risk from obesity and smoking.

The same page has the hopeful half. In that research, 60 to 75 minutes a day of moderate activity offset the effects of too much sitting, and for the most active people, sitting time had little effect on their risk of death. Mayo's first suggestion is modest: take a short break from sitting to standing every 30 minutes. That is an exercise snack in everything but name.

So the snacks earn their place as the habit that stops you sitting for three hours straight. They do not stand in for the hour of real activity the research points to, and I would be wary of anyone who sells them that way. None of the guidance I used for this post measures two-minute snacks on their own.

## Is 10 squats every 45 minutes enough for a desk job?

Ten squats every 45 minutes gets you out of the chair about ten times across a working day, and that alone makes it a good habit. As your only exercise, it falls short. The CDC asks adults for at least 150 minutes of moderate activity a week, such as brisk walking, plus muscle-strengthening on two or more days that works all the major groups: legs, hips, back, abdomen, chest, shoulders and arms.

Squats cover the legs and hips. The back, chest, shoulders and arms are still waiting, and so is the walking.

There is also the knee question. If your knees or back already hurt when you squat, see a doctor first, and do not keep repeating the movement between meetings to push through it. When I assess someone, I look at mobility, past injuries, medicines and how they move today before choosing any exercise. For some people the squat comes later, after a few weeks of mobility work.

## Micro workouts between office meetings

The snacks that last are the ones hidden inside work you already do. The Mayo Clinic suggests standing on phone calls and holding walking meetings, and the rest of this list is how I would fit the idea into an Indian office day:

- Stand for the long calls, especially the ones with your camera off.
- Turn a one-on-one with a colleague into a walk around the floor or the building compound.
- Take the stairs to the next floor, or use the washroom at the far end.
- Walk for five minutes after lunch before you sit down for the 2 pm call.
- Do ten squats or a few wall push-ups beside the desk, if neither one hurts.

## Where the snacks fit in a real week

Many busy professionals I meet treat one gym hour as the job done and forget the walking for the rest of the day. I wrote more about that in [sitting all day but working out for one hour](/blog/sitting-all-day-but-working-out-one-hour/). The snacks fix the second half of the problem. The first half still needs a plan, and the CDC says the 150 minutes can be split into smaller chunks, for example 30 minutes on five days, and that some activity is better than none.

For a founder with about three hours a week, my advice is 30 to 40 minutes of strength work, a 10-minute mobility warm-up and a short finisher, spread across the week. One day can be about 30 minutes of moderate cardio plus mobility. If three sessions do not fit your calendar, we start with the one or two that do, and a session does not have to be a full hour.

Netflix ke liye time hai, body ke liye 15 minute nahi: there is time for Netflix, but not 15 minutes for the body. That is how I put it to people who say they have no time. Seven or eight two-minute snacks across a workday come close to those 15 minutes, and they need no kit or commute.

## One step to start this week

For five working days, set a reminder every 30 minutes, the interval Mayo suggests, or every 45 if your meetings will not allow it. Stand up each time it goes off, even if you only walk to the window and back.
