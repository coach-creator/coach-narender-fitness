Exercising for maintenance after 35 means training so your body keeps doing its daily job without pain: climbing stairs without getting winded, sitting through a long drive without your back complaining, and still having energy at 6 pm. That takes strength training, regular activity and less sitting. A six-pack plan is built for a different goal, and it is usually the one people drop first.

This is general education from a personal trainer, not medical advice. Back pain, chest pain, dizziness, a joint that locks, pain down the leg, or a condition a doctor already follows needs a doctor first. Narender sends clients for imaging and an orthopaedic opinion when that is the right step, then trains with the doctor's guidance.

Say it is January. You sign up for a 12-week shred, eat boiled chicken for a month, and train six days a week. Then a product launch arrives, two weeks of travel follow, and by March the plan is a memory. Your lower back still aches after long calls, exactly as it did in December.

## Why you need strength training to feel normal at 40

Mayo Clinic puts the main reason plainly: lean muscle and strength tend to decline with age, and strength training can help you keep and even increase both. It may also help protect against age-related loss of bone strength. If you never replace the muscle you lose, your body fat percentage will likely go up.

The same page links strength training to better physical function, better balance and a lower risk of falls. It says strength work may improve your ability to do everyday activities and help protect your joints from injury, which helps you stay independent as you age.

That list matches what I see when busy professionals first come to me, usually around 35 to 40 and older. Their energy has dropped. Small daily tasks feel tiring. Stairs leave them winded. Many have stiffness, lower back, knee or neck pain, tight muscles and weak breathing. What they want back is energy for work and a body that does not hurt.

## Training for daily energy vs a six-pack

Most people still start with looks. What they tell me is that they want about 5% of the fat gone and to look better than before. Some even ask who needs to climb, or who is going to run in an emergency anyway. I think that order is backwards. Joint health and function come first, and the looks follow.

The NHS gives you the other side of the trade. People who are active regularly have a lower risk of coronary heart disease, stroke, type 2 diabetes, osteoarthritis, hip fracture and depression, among others. Physical activity can also boost mood, sleep quality and energy and reduce the risk of stress, which is much closer to what busy professionals come to me for.

None of that shows up in a mirror selfie. It shows up in ordinary moments, like carrying the suitcase up to the hotel room without stopping halfway.

## What exercising for maintenance after 35 looks like in a week

The NHS says adults should try to be active every day and aim for at least 150 minutes of activity over a week. Mayo Clinic adds strength training for all major muscle groups at least twice a week, and says you may notice stronger muscles from about 20 to 30 minutes each time.

The NHS adds a warning that fits desk life exactly. You can hit your weekly activity target and still be at risk of ill health if you spend the rest of your time sitting or lying down. It notes that many adults sit for more than 7 hours a day. So an hour in the gym does not finish the job. Walking between calls and getting up from the chair count too.

Inside the sessions, my order is fixed. First an assessment: your history, medicines, mobility and how many days you can truly give. Then about 10 minutes of mobility and attention to your heart and lungs before heavy strength work. Then strength, with a reason for every exercise. Mayo Clinic also advises talking to your healthcare professional first if you have a chronic condition or safety concerns.

## Why people fall off, and how maintenance survives

The six-pack plan asks for everything at once and gives you a finish line. When work gets heavy, it breaks. The usual reason given is time. Netflix ke liye time hai, body ke liye 15 minute nahi: there is time for Netflix but not 15 minutes for the body. Most of the time, no time means not a priority, and people wait until the problem becomes big.

Maintenance asks for less each week but asks for it every week. That is why I ask clients to block training days in the calendar like any other meeting, and to move a missed session instead of dropping it. Progress comes dheere dheere, step by step.
