Glute activation exercises for a desk job are simple strengthening moves for the buttock muscles that weaken when you sit for hours. Cleveland Clinic suggests four: a seated glute squeeze, side-lying leg lifts, glute bridges and clamshells. It pairs them with two habits: getting up regularly, and sitting in a way that does not load your glutes unevenly.

## What is dead butt syndrome from sitting all day?

Dead butt syndrome is the everyday name for what Cleveland Clinic also calls lower cross syndrome, or gluteus medius tendinosis. Its article says long periods of sitting at a desk or in a car are the most common trigger. Sitting gradually weakens the gluteus medius, one of the three main muscles in your buttocks, and that weakness creates an imbalance.

The gluteus medius helps keep your hips and pelvis stable. When it loses strength, Cleveland Clinic says you may feel numbness or tingling in your buttocks, though not in your legs or feet. That explains the awkward first few steps after a long stretch in the chair. Over time, it can grow into buttock, hip and lower back pain.

Picture a Thursday in Gurugram: an hour in traffic to the office, back-to-back calls all morning, lunch at the desk, a 6 pm review and the same drive home. Most of that day is spent in a seat, which is the pattern the article describes.

## How to wake up glutes after sitting

The first fix is to sit less in one go. Cleveland Clinic points out that sitting is not as restful as it feels: one group of muscles is overworking to hold you upright, while another is barely working at all. Standing up and moving gives the sitting muscles a break and switches others on.

Its suggestion is to work at least 20 minutes of standing or walking into every hour at the office, and to set a reminder to get up and stretch about every half hour. That can be as small as taking a phone call on your feet or walking over to a colleague instead of sending a message. A standing desk helps too.

Mayo Clinic makes a similar case for breaking up the workday. It says sitting at a desk for a long time can put stress on muscles in the neck, shoulders and upper back, and that stretches done seated or standing, even during a conference call, can keep you more comfortable. You need no equipment for them.

## Glute activation exercises for a desk job

Cleveland Clinic suggests these four moves to strengthen the glutes and the muscles around them. Its article gives the sets, repetitions and hold times for each, so follow those.

### Seated glute squeeze

Sitting in your chair, tighten your buttock muscles, hold, and then relax. This is the one you can do on a call with your camera off, and nobody in the meeting will know.

### Side-lying leg lifts

Lie on one side with your legs straight and stacked. Slowly raise the top leg towards the ceiling, then lower it with control, and switch sides.

### Glute bridges

Lie on your back with your knees bent and feet flat on the floor, hip-width apart. Lift your hips while tightening your glutes and stomach, only as high as you can go without arching your back, then lower slowly.

### Clamshell

Lie on your side with hips stacked, knees bent and feet together. Keeping your feet touching, lift the top knee so your legs open like a shell, then bring it back down.

The same article adds two stretches for tightness: a seated spinal twist you can do in your desk chair, and a runner's lunge for the hip flexors. Cleveland Clinic's point here is that most people do not hold a stretch long enough, so check its timing before you start.

## How you sit matters too

Cleveland Clinic's posture advice is plain and worth following. Sit up straight with your shoulders relaxed, and use a cushion or a rolled-up towel behind your lower back if that helps you hold it. Keep your feet flat on the floor with your knees at about a right angle, and set the chair so your hips are level with or slightly higher than your knees.

Two habits are worth breaking. Crossing your legs can tilt and rotate the pelvis and put uneven pressure on the glutes. Sitting dead still for hours is the other one, so shift around in your seat, even in a good chair.
