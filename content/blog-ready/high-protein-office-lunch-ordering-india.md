High protein office lunch ordering in India gets simple once you choose the protein first and build the order around it: dal, rajma, chana, paneer, curd, eggs, chicken or fish, then a vegetable dish, then a smaller portion of rice or roti. Most delivery menus in Gurugram can do this. The hard part is deciding before the 1 pm call runs over and someone orders biryani for the whole floor.

## What I see on office lunch plates

The professionals who come to me are smart, well-educated people. Many of them still eat a carb-heavy Indian diet, order a lot of food from outside, and stay low on protein even after they have been told. So the missing piece is rarely information.

Food is fuel, and protein matters. If you are an educated person, you should understand how your body works, and that includes what lands on your desk at lunch. Body aapke work schedule ke hisaab se kaam nahi karti: your body does not run on your work schedule, and it still needs a proper meal on the day the meetings stack up.

## High protein office lunch ordering in India starts before you open the app

Picture a Tuesday. Your 12:30 meeting runs to 1:20, you open the app starving, and the first thing you see is the combo with butter naan, dal makhani, jeera rice and a gulab jamun. At that point the decision is already made for you.

The fix is to decide earlier. The CDC suggests planning meals ahead of time, and the easiest way to plan an ordered lunch is to keep two or three go-to orders saved from places near your office, each built around a protein dish. Then the 1:20 version of you just reorders.

Before you change anything, find out what you actually eat. The CDC's first practical step is to write down everything you eat and drink for a few days in a food diary. One working week of lunch orders is enough. Then look at each order and ask where the protein was, and how big it was next to the rice and roti.

## How to get enough protein when ordering lunch at work

I am not going to give you a gram target from a blog post. How much protein you need depends on your body, your training and any medical condition, and that is part of an assessment. What I can give you is the order of decisions.

Protein first. Pick one clear protein dish, and on most days a second source too: dal with curd, paneer with dal, eggs with a raita, chicken or fish with dal. Then a vegetable dish, because Mayo Clinic's weight-loss advice leans heavily on vegetables, fruits and whole grains, which are filling and low in calories. Then the carbohydrate, in a smaller portion than the restaurant sends by default.

If you keep falling short even when you try, a protein supplement is an option. Supplements are for people who cannot meet their protein from food, and if you have a medical issue, check with your doctor before adding one.

## Indian restaurant swaps for desk workers

None of these turn a lunch into a diet plan. They are small changes on a normal delivery menu, based on Mayo Clinic's general advice to eat fewer refined grains, watch fats that are high in calories even when they are healthy, limit added sugar and pick low-fat dairy.

- Swap a large portion of white rice or naan for a smaller portion, or for brown rice or whole-wheat roti when the place offers it.
- Choose a dry or tandoori dish, or a simple home-style curry, over one made rich with butter and cream.
- Add curd, raita or chaas to the order instead of a sweet lassi or a cola.
- Leave out the dessert that comes with the combo, or share it.
- Add a salad or a sabzi so half the plate is vegetables.

Also watch the office treats. The CDC's suggestion for coworkers who bring in sweets is to bring healthier snacks to share, which is easier than being the person who refuses everything.

## Eat it away from the screen

Mayo Clinic recommends eating mindfully, without watching TV or staring at your phone, because you can eat too much without noticing. At work, the screen is the laptop. Eating through a call with your camera off is the office version of eating in front of the TV.

Take the plate away from the laptop if you can. The CDC suggests walk breaks at work and asking a coworker to join you for a walk at lunch. A short walk after you eat also gets you out of the chair, which most desk days badly need.

## Set two goals, not ten

The CDC advises focusing on two or three specific, realistic goals at a time. "Eat healthier" is not a goal. "Every office lunch has one protein dish and a vegetable dish" is. So is "rice portion halved on weekdays". Pick two, keep them for a few weeks, then add another.

Setbacks will happen. There will be a farewell lunch, a client lunch, a Friday biryani. The CDC's advice is to get back on track as quickly as you can and think about how to handle the same situation next time, which usually means having your saved order ready.

## When to get help

If you are concerned about your weight or have questions about your medicines, the CDC says to talk with your health care provider. Your provider can also refer you to a registered dietitian. A coach can help you fit food habits and training into a busy calendar.
