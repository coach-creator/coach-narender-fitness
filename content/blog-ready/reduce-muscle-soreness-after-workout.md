To reduce muscle soreness after workout sessions, plan before the session starts. In the way I train people, that means a proper warm-up, a load that builds dheere dheere, slowly and step by step, and a plan that knows what your calendar looks like the next day. If what you feel afterwards is pain rather than stiffness, stop and get it checked.

This is general education from a personal trainer, not medical advice. Back pain, chest pain, dizziness, a joint that locks, pain down the leg, or a condition a doctor already follows needs a doctor first. Narender sends clients for imaging and an orthopaedic opinion when that is the right step, then trains with the doctor's guidance.

Say you did your first real leg session in months on Monday evening. On Tuesday you have back-to-back meetings, and every time you get up from the conference room chair your thighs remind you about Monday. Then comes a two-hour review where you would rather stand at the back than sit down again.

## Leg day soreness when you sit at a desk all day

That stiff, heavy feeling after a new or harder session is what most people call DOMS, short for delayed onset muscle soreness. I am not going to give you a timeline or a quick fix for it here. What I can tell you is how I plan training so that soreness does not take over your workday.

The pattern I work hardest to avoid is jumping straight into heavy strength work. Before I add heavy load, I want to understand your muscle imbalances, give about 10 minutes to mobility, and look after your heart and lungs. A busy professional who goes from months of sitting to a heavy leg day has skipped all three.

There is also a trainer problem. Some sessions are built to leave you wrecked, because wrecked feels like value. Thaka ke bhej dete hain: they tire you out and send you home. I say the same about sweat, which is just your body's temperature going up and it cooling itself down. Feeling destroyed the next day does not prove the session was right for you either.

## How to reduce muscle soreness after a workout, starting before it

Start with the warm-up. The NHS says to warm up properly before exercising to prevent injury and make your workouts more effective, and that the warm-up should take at least 6 minutes, longer if you feel the need. Its page sets out a simple routine you can follow at home or in a hotel gym. To be clear, the NHS frames the warm-up around injury and performance. It does not promise less soreness, and I will not either.

In my plans, about 10 minutes of mobility comes before any strength work, every time.

Then build the load slowly. A simple way to keep it gradual is to change one thing at a time: a little more weight, or a few more reps, or one extra set, and not all three in the same week. If you want the longer version of why mobility comes before load, read [mobility before heavy lifting](/blog/mobility-before-heavy-lifting/).

## Plan the hard sessions around your calendar

Soreness matters more when the next day is long. If Wednesday is a flight to Bengaluru and a full afternoon of meetings, Tuesday night is not the night for your hardest leg session. Put the heavier sessions before lighter days, and keep mobility or upper-body work for the evening before a packed one.

This is why I ask about your days during the assessment. How many days you can really train, and what those days look like, shape the plan as much as your body does.

## When soreness is really pain

Do not push through pain to prove a point. The NHS says to stop exercising immediately if you feel any pain or become unwell, and to get help if you are concerned about symptoms or they do not go away. It also advises getting advice from a healthcare professional before exercising if you have a health problem, an injury or any symptoms.

A joint that locks or pain that runs down a leg is not workout soreness. See a doctor before you train that area again.

## The one step for this week

Before your next session, do a warm-up of at least 6 minutes and change only one thing from your last workout. Note how your body feels the next working day.
