If you feel too exhausted after a personal trainer session every time, the session is probably asking more than your body can recover from right now. Hard work is part of training. Leaving wiped out, sleeping badly and dreading the next one is a sign the load is wrong for where you are, and for busy people it is often the reason they quit.

This is general education from a personal trainer, not medical advice. Back pain, chest pain, dizziness, a joint that locks, pain down the leg, or a condition a doctor already follows needs a doctor first. Narender sends clients for imaging and an orthopaedic opinion when that is the right step, then trains with the doctor's guidance.

Picture a Tuesday. Your trainer comes at 7 am and runs you through jumps, burpees and circuits until you are lying on the mat. You are soaked and proud of it. By 11 am you are dragging through a board review, by evening your legs feel like concrete, and on Thursday you cancel with "work came up". By the third week, you stop booking.

## Why feeling too exhausted after a session makes busy people quit

Mayo Clinic describes this pattern almost exactly. Many people start a fitness programme with a lot of energy, work out too long or too hard, and then give up when their muscles and joints become sore or injured. Its advice is to start slowly, build up gradually, and plan time between sessions for your body to recover.

For a busy professional, the cost of a wiped-out session does not stay in the gym. It shows up in the 11 am meeting, in your patience at home, and in your sleep. You did not hire a trainer to lose half a working day after every session. When training costs that much, the calendar wins, and the habit dies.

I say this about some trainers: thaka ke bhej dete hain. They tire you out and send you home. People often ask for it, because basic work feels dull and they want to be entertained. I will not call every trainer wrong, sabko galat nahi kahunga. But any session can tire you out; the skill is in building you up without wrecking your week.

> Aap class le rahe ho, entertainment ke liye nahi aa rahe.

You are taking a class, not coming to be entertained. A good session teaches your body something and leaves you able to come back for the next one.

## Should a personal trainer make you sore every workout?

No. MedlinePlus, a US government health site, lists the signs of pushing too hard for too long. They include not being able to perform at the same level, needing longer rest, feeling tired, trouble sleeping, sore muscles or heavy limbs, overuse injuries, mood swings and losing motivation. If that list sounds like your week, MedlinePlus suggests cutting back or resting completely for one or two weeks, and seeing your health care provider if you are still tired after that.

Sweat is not proof either. Sweating is just your body's temperature going up and it cooling itself down. A soaked shirt tells you your body heated up, not whether the work was right for you. I have written more about this in [does sweating mean a good workout](/blog/does-sweating-mean-a-good-workout/).

## The difference between a hard workout and a smart workout

A smart workout still has hard sets in it. The difference is that every exercise has a reason, and the load matches what your body can handle today. Mayo Clinic's guidance points the same way: warm up, then build to a pace you can keep for 5 to 10 minutes without getting overly tired, and raise your activity by no more than 10% a week. The NHS suggests a warm-up of at least six minutes and says to stop immediately if you feel pain or become unwell.

So ask your trainer one question about each exercise: what is this for, and why? A good coach answers in a sentence. If the only answer is that it will make you sweat, or that it was in a video, that is a sign the plan was not built for you. Some trainers take five exercises off YouTube in the morning and give every client the same pattern, with no medical history. I have seen it happen.

My own sessions start with an assessment: what hurts, which medicines you take, what activity you did before and now, how many days you can really train, and how you are coping with stress. Then mobility before heavy strength, and the heart and lungs built up dheere dheere, little by little. The aim is that you finish, go to work, and turn up for the next one.
