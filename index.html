Who I work with

Most of the professionals I meet are smart, successful, and tired. After 35, energy often drops, the back or knees start hurting, and climbing stairs feels harder than it should. They've usually tried a gym, an app, or a trainer who made them jump around until they were drenched in sweat.

Sweat is just your body cooling down. It tells you nothing about whether the workout helped.

How I coach

  1. Assessment first

    Your goals, past injuries, medicines, stress, how you move, and how many days you can really train.

  2. A plan built for you

    No random exercises. Strength, mobility and heart health, planned around your body and your calendar.

  3. The reason behind every exercise

    You'll understand what each exercise does and why, so you can look after your own body.

  4. Doctors when needed

    If something needs an X‑ray, MRI or an orthopaedic opinion, I'll say so and work with your doctor.

  5. Mind as well as body

    Stress and sleep decide whether you stay consistent, so they're part of the plan too.

Client stories

Three clients, shared with their permission. Names kept private.

Disc bulge

From “don’t bend” to squats and jogging

A software developer in her early thirties had a disc bulge from long hours of sitting. Her doctors had told her not to bend or run, and she was scared to move. We started with an assessment and small, safe steps, plus yoga and meditation for the mental side. Today she squats, does lunges, walks and jogs.

Frozen shoulder

From a frozen arm to a 30 kg press

A senior professional, diabetic, who used to play tennis and golf, had lived with a frozen shoulder for three years. We got an MRI and an orthopaedic opinion first, then worked on mobility and strength alongside his doctor. In six to eight months he went from not lifting his arm straight to pressing about 30 kg and doing push-ups.

Consistency

The client who couldn't keep a routine

A busy professional kept missing sessions. We booked fixed days in the calendar like meetings, and I came to the house. Early dizziness turned out to be poor sleep and nutrition, so we fixed those and found the time by cutting late-night screen time. Now a missed session gets done in the evening.

Questions

Where do the sessions happen?

At your home anywhere in Gurugram, or online if you're travelling or outside Gurugram.

What happens in the first session?

An assessment: your goals, past injuries, medicines, stress, how you move, and how many days a week you can realistically train. You leave knowing what your body needs next, even if you don't continue.

I have back pain or an old injury. Can I still train?

Usually, yes. I work around pain and injuries, and when something needs a doctor or a scan, I say so and work alongside your orthopaedic doctor.

I only have a few hours a week. Is that enough?

Yes. With three hours a week, each session can cover 30 to 40 minutes of strength work plus mobility, planned around your calendar.
